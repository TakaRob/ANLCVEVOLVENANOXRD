General Audience Abstract 

Takaji Robson 

During my SULI appointment at Argonne National Laboratory, I worked on improving how scientists analyze X-ray data from promising solar-cell materials. My research took place at the Advanced Photon Source, a U.S. Department of Energy user facility that provides extremely bright X-rays for studying matter at very small scales. I focused on perovskite-halide thin films, which are attractive for next-generation solar cells because they can be highly efficient and potentially less expensive to manufacture than traditional materials. 

To understand these materials, researchers at the APS-19ID beamline use nano-focused X-ray diffraction to map the size and orientation of tiny crystal grains within a film. However, each scan can produce hundreds of thousands of images, creating a major data-processing challenge. During my appointment, I developed xrd-app, a command-line software tool that automates this analysis. The workflow compresses raw data, detects diffraction signals, and links related measurements across neighboring scan points to reconstruct grain-level structural features. I also used methods such as spatial binning and clustering to improve the detection of weaker signals and make the analysis faster and more reliable. 

Applying this tool to scans from perovskite solar-cell samples showed that processing conditions affect crystal structure. Samples handled outside a glove box displayed a stronger preferred crystal orientation, and adding a small amount of deionized water increased the average size of grains with that orientation. These findings help connect fabrication methods to material structure, which is important for improving device performance. 

This work supports the Department of Energy mission by advancing materials research for clean energy technologies and by improving the computational tools needed to study them efficiently. Through this experience, I strengthened my skills in scientific programming, data analysis, and communicating research to broad audiences, while gaining firsthand experience in a national laboratory environment and collaborative energy research.
