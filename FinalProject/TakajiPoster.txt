TITLE
===================================================================
High resolution structural mapping of perovskite thin films (ISN 19-ID)
Resolving (111) and (001) microstructure via automated nano-XRD analysis
Takaji Robson — X-ray Science Division

===================================================================
ABSTRACT
===================================================================
Nano-focused X-ray diffraction (nano-XRD) at the APS 19-ID beamline enables
spatially resolved characterization of crystal grain size and orientation in
semiconductor thin films. However, each scan produces on the order of [10^?]
diffraction frames, making quick analysis demanding. To address this, we
developed xrd-app, a command-line computer vision pipeline that compresses raw
HDF5 data, detects Bragg peaks frame by frame, and links neighboring detections
across space to reconstruct grain features, enabling fast and automated analysis
of key structural info.

To manage the large data volume, we implemented two key design strategies:
 - Utilize CVEvolve: generates tuned algorithms for peak detection.
 - Spatial Binning: sums neighboring frames and raises per-position signal to
   noise (SNR) linearly with bin size.
 - Contouring: a Union-Find + Gaussian-profile filtering that merges scattered
   detections into coherent grain outlines.

We applied this workflow to 6 focused scans across three perovskite-halide solar
cells. Results show that No Glove Box promotes a preferred (001) grain
orientation relative to (111), while 5% Cl treatment increases the average size
of (001) grains.

===================================================================
BINNING
===================================================================
Median SNR increases with bin size, improving peak detection. Binning also
stabilizes grain outlines by smoothing over scan-to-scan offsets introduced by
the S-shaped raster trajectory. In the un-binned case, these offsets can appear
as holes or horizontal disconnects in the grain maps.

Binning sums neighboring frames before detections, reducing spatial resolution
for SNR. Device grid reduced (e.g. 151x145 to 51x49).

===================================================================
CONTOURING
===================================================================
Detections along the detector image are characterized by their azimuthal
orientation, intensity, and radial breadth.

Links per-position detections into grains, combining a Union-Find with a
Gaussian-profile filter to identify grains with a bright monotonic center and
reject noise or fragmented detections.

This contouring step enables less restrictive detection but assumes a minimum
grain size.

===================================================================
XRD-APP RESULTS
===================================================================
Nano-XRD experiments can generate large datasets; for example, Scan_0203
contains 25,170 frames. The three devices in this study were measured across 56
scans, ranging from 80-200 GB. By combining spatial binning with contouring,
this workflow provides an automated detection approach that allows less
restrictive peak-detection algorithms by assuming grain persistence across
neighboring pixels.

DEVICE CONCLUSIONS
===================================================================
No Glove Box preserves (001) grain dominance over (111).
No_GB devices show a reduced (001):(111) size ratio and larger (111) grains.
5% Cl treatment increases the average size and total area share of (001) grains.

No Glove Box preserves (001) grain dominance. In No_GB devices the 001:111 size
ratio collapses from ~3.4 to ~0.9, and the largest (111) grain doubles in size
compared to the overall device area. 5% Cl strengthens (001) dominance, the
total summed area ratio climbing from 2.9 to 4.6 over (111). Mean (001) size
rises while (111) remains relatively unchanged.
